Financial Assistance for Aging-in-Place Home Improvements

Several programs may help pay for home modifications like walk-in showers, grab bars, and accessible remodels. This guide explains the major categories, what each may cover, and where to confirm the details for your state.

By the Aging at Home Advisor editorial process Program details last checked July 2026 Sources

Before you read: programs vary by state, eligibility is never guaranteed, and coverage rules change. Always confirm details with the relevant program. Aging at Home Advisor is not a government agency.

Medicare

FEDERAL

Original Medicare focuses on medical care, not home renovation — but it’s the first program most families ask about, so it’s worth understanding the boundaries. Read the full guide →

WHAT IT MAY COVER Generally does not cover bathroom remodels or walk-in tubs. Some durable medical equipment (like certain commode or bath safety items) may be covered when prescribed.
WHO MAY QUALIFY Adults 65+ and some younger people with disabilities who are enrolled in Medicare.
WHERE TO APPLY Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE; ask your plan directly about specific items.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS Home modifications are usually excluded. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited supplemental benefits — rules differ by plan and year.

Medicaid & state waiver programs

STATE + FEDERAL

Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS) waivers are one of the most common sources of help for accessibility modifications that let people stay in their homes. Read the full guide →

WHAT IT MAY COVER Environmental or home accessibility modifications — which can include roll-in showers, grab bars, and similar safety changes — in states whose waivers include them.
WHO MAY QUALIFY People who meet their state’s Medicaid financial and functional criteria; waiver slots are often limited.
WHERE TO APPLY Your state Medicaid agency or Area Agency on Aging.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS Every state’s waiver list is different, waiting lists are common, and annual caps may apply.

Veterans Affairs benefits

VETERANS

The VA runs several grant programs specifically designed to make veterans’ homes safer and more accessible. Read the full guide →

WHAT IT MAY COVER Grants such as HISA, SAH, and SHA may help pay for bathroom accessibility modifications, depending on the grant and the veteran’s situation.
WHO MAY QUALIFY Veterans — and for some grants, veterans with service-connected disabilities.
WHERE TO APPLY VA.gov or a Veterans Service Officer (VSO) can help with the application.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS Each grant has its own eligibility rules and dollar limits, and processing can take time.

State & local programs

STATE / LOCAL

Many states, counties, and cities run home repair or modification programs for older adults, often funded through community development grants.

WHAT IT MAY COVER Varies widely — from minor safety repairs and grab bars to larger accessibility projects.
WHO MAY QUALIFY Often based on age, income, and homeownership; each program sets its own rules.
WHERE TO APPLY Your Area Agency on Aging (eldercare.acl.gov) is the best starting point.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS Funding is limited and cyclical; programs open and close as budgets allow.

Nonprofit assistance

NONPROFIT

National and local nonprofits help income-qualified homeowners with safety repairs and modifications, sometimes at no cost.

WHAT IT MAY COVER Commonly smaller-scope safety work: grab bars, railings, ramps, and minor bathroom safety improvements.
WHO MAY QUALIFY Usually income-qualified homeowners; some programs prioritize older adults or veterans.
WHERE TO APPLY Organizations such as Rebuilding Together and local affiliates; availability varies by area.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS Waitlists are common and large remodels are rarely covered.

Home improvement financing

PRIVATE

When assistance programs don’t apply, families often compare financing options to spread the cost of a project over time.

WHAT IT MAY COVER Any project — through home equity loans or lines of credit, government-insured renovation loans, or contractor financing.
WHO MAY QUALIFY Based on credit, income, and home equity; terms vary by lender.
WHERE TO APPLY Banks, credit unions, and lenders; compare rates and total cost carefully.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS Financing adds interest cost and uses your home or credit as the basis — consider advice from a trusted financial professional.

Tax-related considerations

TAX

In some situations, home modifications made for medical reasons may have tax implications worth discussing with a professional.

WHAT IT MAY COVER Certain medically necessary home improvements may qualify as medical expense deductions in some circumstances.
WHO MAY QUALIFY Depends on medical necessity, itemizing, and IRS thresholds — individual situations vary.
WHERE TO APPLY Discuss with a qualified tax professional; see IRS guidance on medical expenses.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS This is general information, not tax advice. Rules change and value-adding improvements are treated differently.

Keep in mind

Programs vary significantly by state — what a neighbor received elsewhere may not apply to you. Eligibility is never guaranteed, even when you appear to meet the criteria. Coverage rules and funding levels change from year to year. Always confirm details directly with the relevant program before signing a contract.
