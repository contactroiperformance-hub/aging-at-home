Bathroom Accessibility Options for a Safer Home

The bathroom is one of the most important rooms to adapt for long-term comfort and safety. Small changes — or a well-planned project — can make daily routines easier and reduce the risk of falls, while keeping the room attractive and comfortable.

Safety features worth considering

Most projects combine several of these. A good installer will help you choose what fits your bathroom and routine.

Grab bars Low or zero threshold Built-in seating Slip-resistant flooring Handheld showerhead Accessible controls Improved lighting Lever-style handles

Planning checklist

Measure the bathroom and note the door width
List who uses the bathroom and any mobility considerations
Decide between showering, bathing, or both
Set a comfortable budget range before requesting quotes
Review financial assistance options for your state
Plan where you’ll bathe during installation

Financial assistance may be available

Medicaid waiver programs, veterans’ benefits, and state or local home modification programs may help with some projects. Eligibility varies by program, state, personal circumstances, and project type.

Frequently asked questions

Which project reduces fall risk the most?

It depends on the person and the bathroom. Low-threshold entry, seating, grab bars, and slip-resistant flooring do the most work — all four project types can include them. This is general guidance, not medical advice.

How long do these projects take?

A tub-to-shower conversion or walk-in tub installation often takes a few days. A full accessible remodel typically takes several weeks.

Can I keep my bathtub and still make the bathroom safer?

Yes. Grab bars, transfer benches, slip-resistant surfaces, and improved lighting can meaningfully improve safety without replacing the tub.

Will an accessible bathroom hurt my home’s value?

Well-designed accessible bathrooms are increasingly seen as an asset, especially designs that look premium rather than clinical.

Is financial assistance available for these projects?

Sometimes. Medicaid waiver programs, veterans’ benefits, and state or local programs may help. Eligibility varies by program, state, and personal circumstances.
