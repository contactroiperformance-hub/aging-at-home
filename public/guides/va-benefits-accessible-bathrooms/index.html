VA Benefits for Accessible Bathroom Modifications

Eligible Veterans and service members may be able to use certain Department of Veterans Affairs benefits for medically necessary or disability-related home adaptations, including some accessible bathroom improvements. The principal programs are HISA (Home Improvements and Structural Alterations), SAH (Specially Adapted Housing), SHA (Special Home Adaptation), and TRA (Temporary Residence Adaptation).

These programs serve different purposes with different eligibility, ownership, disability, application, and funding rules. A walk-in tub or bathroom remodel is not automatically covered simply because the applicant is a Veteran — approval comes before work, not after.

HISA and bathroom modifications

HISA supports medically necessary improvements or structural alterations to a Veteran’s primary residence. Per VA’s HISA guidance, relevant purposes include access to essential lavatory and sanitary facilities, roll-in showers, access to bathroom sinks or counters, plumbing or electrical changes required by installation of home medical equipment, and entry or exit adaptations where applicable.

HISA is not a general bathroom-remodeling grant: it follows documented medical need at the primary residence, prescribed or approved by a VA physician.

What HISA does not cover

Spa or hot tubs Jacuzzi-type tubs Routine home repairs Exterior decking Certain removable equipment Other work excluded in current VA guidance

Note the nuance: exclusions cover spa and Jacuzzi-type tubs, but that doesn’t mean every walk-in tub is automatically classified as one. Ask the local VA Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service how the proposed product and scope will be classified before planning around it.

HISA application requirements

The current package may include the following — use the current VA form and your local facility’s instructions:

A prescription written or approved by a VA physician
A description of the proposed project
Diagnosis and medical justification
Completed VA Form 10-0103
An itemized estimate covering labor, materials, permits, and inspections
A photograph of the unimproved area
Owner authorization for a rented residence
A possible site inspection

SAH, SHA, and TRA housing grants

SAH helps eligible Veterans and service members with certain qualifying service-connected disabilities buy, build, or adapt a permanent home they own or will own; it can be used in multiple installments within current program rules, and bathroom modifications may be part of a broader approved adapted-housing project. SHA is a separate grant for different qualifying service-connected disabilities; under current rules the Veteran or a family member may own or plan to own the home. TRA may be available when a person who qualifies for SAH or SHA is temporarily living in a family member’s home that needs adaptation — it is not a general renter benefit.

Not all Veterans with mobility limitations are eligible: each grant turns on specific qualifying service-connected disabilities determined by VA.

Frequently asked questions

Does VA pay for accessible bathroom remodeling?

For eligible Veterans, certain VA programs may fund medically necessary or disability-related bathroom adaptations — HISA for medical-necessity alterations, and SAH/SHA within broader adapted-housing projects. Nothing is automatic: eligibility, documentation, and prior approval all apply.

Can HISA pay for a roll-in shower?

Roll-in showers are among the purposes VA lists for HISA, when medically necessary and approved for the primary residence. The application and approval come first.

Does HISA cover walk-in tubs?

HISA excludes spa and Jacuzzi-type tubs, but that doesn’t automatically classify every walk-in tub as one. Ask the local VA Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service how the specific product and scope would be classified before planning around it.

What is the difference between HISA and SAH?

HISA is a medical benefit through VA health care for medically necessary alterations, with lifetime limits. SAH is a housing grant for Veterans with specific qualifying service-connected disabilities, supporting larger buy/build/adapt projects up to a fiscal-year maximum.

What is the difference between SAH and SHA?

They serve different sets of qualifying service-connected disabilities, with different maximums (FY 2026: $126,526 for SAH, $25,350 for SHA) and slightly different ownership rules — under SHA, a family member may own the home.

Can a Veteran use HISA in a rented home?

Possibly — HISA applications for rented residences generally require the owner’s written authorization. Confirm with the local VA office.

What documentation is needed for HISA?

Typically: a VA physician’s prescription or approval, the project description, diagnosis and medical justification, VA Form 10-0103, an itemized estimate, a photo of the unimproved area, and owner authorization if renting. Use the current form and local instructions.

Should work begin before VA approval?

No. Approval should come before work — starting early can jeopardize funding. Confirm the sequence with the VA office handling the application.

How much does HISA provide?

Lifetime benefits of up to $6,800 for certain service-connected or otherwise qualifying circumstances, and up to $2,000 for certain other eligible non-service-connected circumstances — verified against VA program information as of July 2026. Confirm current figures with VA.

What are the FY 2026 SAH and SHA maximums?

For fiscal year 2026: $126,526 for SAH and $25,350 for SHA, per VA.gov. These adjust by fiscal year — always check the current year’s figure.

Can TRA modify a family member’s home?

Yes — that’s its purpose: temporary residence adaptation for an SAH- or SHA-qualified person living temporarily in a family member’s home, up to FY 2026 maximums of $50,961 (via SAH) or $9,100 (via SHA).

Can more than one VA benefit be used?

In some circumstances, programs serve different needs and may both apply over time — for example, HISA for a medically necessary alteration and a housing grant for a broader adaptation. The VA offices administering each benefit determine what’s permitted.

Does a contractor submit the VA application?

No — the Veteran (often with help from a Veterans Service Officer) applies. Contractors provide estimates and perform approved work; they do not decide eligibility, and no contractor can guarantee VA approval.

Where should a Veteran ask for help?

The local VA medical center’s Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service for HISA; VA housing assistance (VA Form 26-4555) for SAH/SHA/TRA; and accredited Veterans Service Officers, who help with applications at no charge.
