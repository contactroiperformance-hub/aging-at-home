How Much Does a Walk-In Shower Cost?

A professionally installed walk-in shower commonly requires a planning budget of approximately $6,000 to $12,000, based on Angi’s 2026 cost data. Simpler prefab installations may cost less, while large custom, tiled, frameless, or curbless showers can reach $20,000 or more.

Published averages vary: current national estimates place the typical project around $9,000–$12,000 depending on scope and data source, and This Old House’s 2026 research spans roughly $4,000–$20,000 from simple to highly customized. Treat all of these as planning ranges — not quotes.

Walk-in shower cost at a glance

Prefabricated shower $4,000–$7,000 Kit base and wall system in a standard footprint, plumbing reused.
Semi-custom installation $6,000–$10,000 Quality panels or partial tile, seat, grab bars, better glass.
Custom tiled walk-in shower $9,000–$15,000 Tiled walls and floor, custom pan, frameless glass options.
Curbless accessible shower $12,000–$20,000 Recessed floor, linear drain, extended waterproofing, roll-in capable.

How we build these figures: published estimates differ because sources use different project scopes, contractor databases, material assumptions, labor markets, bathroom configurations, installation requirements, and geographic samples — there is no single correct national price. We anchor on Angi’s 2026 typical range ($6,000 to $12,000) and use This Old House’s wider 2026 range ($4,000–$20,000) for the low and high scenarios. Cost by shower type

Prefab shower kit Lowest cost
Molded base plus wall panels; fast install, limited sizes.
Custom shower pan Moderate add
A site-built, waterproofed base in any dimension — the foundation for tiled floors.
Tiled shower Higher
Tile on walls and often floor; labor hours are the main driver.
Doorless shower Varies
Saves glass hardware but needs a layout and size that contain water.
Frameless-glass shower Premium add
Heavy tempered panels with minimal hardware — a significant upgrade line.
Curbless shower Highest
Floor recessed to drain without a curb; structural and drainage work included.
Roll-in shower Highest
A curbless design with entry width, space, controls, and seating planned for wheelchair use.

A shower isn’t wheelchair accessible just because it has no door. Roll-in access depends on the entry width, turning space, controls, seating, and the bathroom around it — and compliance claims require the full installed design to be evaluated against the applicable requirements.

Cost by size

Bigger showers cost more in every direction at once: more waterproofing, more wall material, more flooring, more glass, more labor hours, and more careful drainage planning to keep water moving toward the drain across a larger floor. Size also interacts with accessibility — generous dimensions help with seating, assistance, and mobility devices — but no single dimension makes a shower ADA-compliant on its own.

Materials

From most economical to most premium: fiberglass and acrylic systems install quickly and clean easily; solid-surface panels add a seamless upscale look without grout; ceramic and porcelain tile bring design range with more labor; natural stone tops the scale for both cost and upkeep. The shower base is its own decision — a molded base is economical and reliable, while a custom-tiled or trench-drain floor supports curbless designs at a higher price. Glass is a major swing item: framed doors are moderate, frameless glass is a significant upgrade, and doorless or curtain designs can cost almost nothing.

Labor and installation

Demolition Framing Waterproofing Plumbing Drainage Tiling Glass installation Fixture installation Electrical and lighting where required

Waterproofing is the step that determines whether the shower still works in ten years. It’s invisible when finished — which is exactly why you should ask every bidder to explain their system before comparing prices.

Curbless and barrier-free cost considerations

A curbless shower looks simple and is anything but. The floor must be recessed or rebuilt so the showering area slopes to the drain below the level of the rest of the room — often with a linear drain along one edge. That means structural work in the floor, extensive waterproofing that extends beyond the shower zone, careful transitions to the bathroom flooring, and honest planning about water containment. In homes where the floor structure can’t be recessed, a very low threshold or a collapsible water dam can deliver most of the benefit for less. This is the main reason curbless projects occupy the top of the cost range.

Glass door, curtain, or doorless design

Framed or semi-frameless glass is the middle path on price and gives reliable water containment. Frameless glass is the premium option — beautiful, more expensive, and dependent on precise installation. A weighted curtain is the least costly, easiest for caregiver assistance, and simple to replace. Doorless designs eliminate hardware entirely but demand a layout and drainage plan that genuinely contains water — they work best in larger showers. None of these choices is “right”; they trade budget, looks, and everyday practicality differently.

How to budget: three sample scenarios

Illustrative planning scenarios — not quotes:

Focused prefab project ≈ $4,000–$7,000 Standard footprint, kit base and panels, curtain or framed door, seat and grab bars specified up front.
Mid-range accessible walk-in shower ≈ $8,000–$13,000 Low threshold, solid-surface or partial tile, fold-down seat, reinforced bars, handheld shower, semi-frameless glass.
High-customization curbless remodel ≈ $14,000–$20,000+ Recessed floor with linear drain, full tile, frameless glass or doorless layout, lighting and ventilation upgrades.

Frequently asked questions

What is the average price of a walk-in shower?

Published averages differ by source and scope. Angi’s 2026 data puts a typical installed project around $6,000–$12,000, with several national estimates clustering near $9,000–$12,000. This Old House’s 2026 range runs roughly $4,000–$20,000 from simple prefab to highly customized. All are planning ranges, not quotes.

How much does a prefab walk-in shower cost?

Roughly $4,000–$7,000 installed as a national planning estimate, assuming a standard footprint and reused plumbing. Prefab systems save on both materials and labor hours.

How much does a tiled walk-in shower cost?

Custom tiled showers commonly plan around $9,000–$15,000, driven mostly by labor: waterproofing prep, tile setting, and grouting. Large-format tile can trim labor and later grout maintenance.

Is a curbless shower more expensive?

Yes — typically the most expensive configuration, planning around $12,000–$20,000. The floor must be recessed or rebuilt to slope to the drain, with extended waterproofing and careful transitions to the rest of the bathroom.

Can a walk-in shower fit in a small bathroom?

Usually yes. Compact bases, sliding or curtain entries, and corner layouts are made for small rooms — converting the existing tub alcove is the most common approach. Seating and door swing need the most planning in tight spaces.

Does a walk-in shower need a door?

No. Doorless and curtain designs work well — and make caregiver assistance easier — provided the layout and drainage genuinely contain water. Doorless designs favor larger showers.

How long does installation take?

Prefab installations often take a few days; custom tiled projects a week or more; curbless remodels longer still, especially when floor structure or permits are involved.

What accessibility features should be added?

The high-value set: a low or zero threshold, seating, grab bars anchored into reinforced walls, a handheld showerhead, reachable anti-scald controls, slip-resistant flooring, and good lighting — chosen for the specific user, not installed generically.

Does Medicare cover walk-in showers?

Original Medicare covers defined medical services and qualifying durable medical equipment; permanent bathroom remodeling is not listed as standard covered DME. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental benefits that vary by plan and year — verify with your plan.

Can Medicaid help with an accessible shower?

In some states, yes — Medicaid home- and community-based services programs may cover home accessibility modifications. Eligibility, covered work, and limits vary by state and program; confirm with your state Medicaid agency and see our financial assistance guide.
